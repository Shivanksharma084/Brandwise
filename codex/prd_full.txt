1. Executive Summary
Organisations now publish through many hands: employees, agencies, freelancers and AI
writing tools. Each source can drift from the approved tone, terminology, facts and visual
identity. Brandwise is a review layer that sits between draft and publish. It checks content
against the organisation's own approved guidelines, fact register and asset library. It
explains every finding by citing the exact rule or source behind it, proposes a fix that
keeps the creator's intent, and routes the decision to a human through a role-based
approval workflow. It also learns where people keep failing and where the guidelines
themselves are unclear.
One-line pitch: A spell-checker for brand trust: it catches off-brand, unsupported or
inaccessible content before it goes public, shows its working, and never overrides a human.
Three named improvements (the Round 2 and 3 rubrics score "the three improvements
you named", so we commit to them now):
1. Source-grounded, explainable findings. Every flag shows the rule or fact it came from,
a quoted excerpt, a confidence level and a plain-language reason. No source means no
flag.
2. Claim and fact verification. Numbers, superlatives, price/offer statements and health
or financial claims are matched against an approved-facts register with owners and
expiry dates. Unsupported or stale claims are flagged.
3. Guideline-gap insights. Repeated overrides, disputed flags and recurring mistakes are
clustered to show which guideline is ambiguous, outdated or missing. This turns the
tool into a governance feedback loop for the brand team.

Brand inconsistency and false or unsupported claims reach the public because review
happens late, manually, or not at all. Guidelines live in PDFs that nobody reads at the
moment of writing. Reviewers become a bottleneck, and AI-generated drafts increase
volume faster than humans can check.

4.2 Design principles (from the problem statement)
1. Explain, do not just reject. Each finding includes what, where, why and a suggested
alternative.
2. Ground in approved sources. Findings cite a guideline clause, fact record or asset.
Ungrounded AI opinions are labelled "advisory" and cannot block publishing.
3. Human control. AI suggests; people decide. Only authorised roles can approve,
override or waive.
4. Preserve intent. Suggestions are minimal edits that keep meaning and voice.
5. Protect confidential material. Tenant isolation, minimal data sent to external models,
clear AI disclosure.
6. Learn from repetition. Track patterns to improve guidelines, not to rank or punish
individuals.
4.3 End-to-end workflow
1. Set up the brand workspace. Admin imports guidelines (PDF/DOCX/Markdown),
which are parsed into atomic, citable rules (tone, terminology, messaging, legal
disclaimers, visual rules, accessibility). Admin also loads the approved facts register

(statistic, value, source, owner, valid-until), a terminology list (preferred, banned, with
examples), and assets (logos, palettes, fonts, templates).
2. Creator submits content (text, image, PDF/DOCX, or social post with image) with
metadata: channel, audience, campaign, and whether it is AI-assisted.
3. Automated review pipeline runs deterministic checks first, then retrieval-grounded AI
checks (see §6). Output: a list of findings with severity, category, evidence, source
citation, confidence and suggested fix.
4. Creator triage. Creator accepts, edits, rejects with reason, or asks for help on each
finding. Suggested fixes appear as tracked changes, never auto-applied.
5. Submit for approval. Content goes to a manager and, where rules require (for example
health claims), an authorised reviewer. Comments, @mentions and version history are
kept.
6. Decision. Approve, request changes, or approve with documented override. Overrides
require a reason and are logged.
7. Export and publish handoff. Approved version is locked and exported with a review
certificate (who, when, which guideline version). Direct publishing integrations are
future scope.
8. Insights loop. Dashboard shows top recurring issues, rules with high override or
dispute rates ("unclear guideline" candidates), stale facts nearing expiry, and trends
over time.

6. Technical Plan
6.1 Proposed architecture
Frontend: Next.js (React) with Tailwind; rich-text editor (TipTap) for annotated content
and tracked-change suggestions.
Backend API: FastAPI (Python) for the review pipeline and workflow; REST with typed
schemas (Pydantic).
Database: PostgreSQL for users, roles, rules, facts, content versions, findings,
comments and audit log; pgvector for embedding search over guideline chunks and
facts.
File storage: S3-compatible storage (MinIO locally, or Supabase Storage) with per-
tenant prefixes.
Background jobs: Celery or RQ with Redis for ingestion and long reviews.

Auth: Supabase Auth or Auth.js with JWT; roles enforced in the API and by row-level

6.2 Review pipeline (the technical core)
1. Parse content into text blocks, claims and regions (PDF/DOCX with PyMuPDF and
python-docx; OCR for images with Tesseract if needed).
2. Deterministic layer: terminology match, regex for numbers and prices, required-
disclaimer presence, WCAG contrast calculation, colour extraction against the brand
palette, missing alt text, readability score.
3. Retrieval layer: embed content spans and retrieve the top relevant rules and facts
(guideline chunks, fact register, approved examples).
4. LLM judgement layer (Claude API or equivalent, behind an adapter): for each span plus
retrieved evidence, return structured JSON: {violation: bool, rule_id,
evidence_quote, explanation, confidence, suggested_fix} . The model may only
cite retrieved items; outputs that cite nothing are downgraded to advisory.
5. Claim verification: extract claims (numbers, comparatives, superlatives,
health/finance/legal claims); match to the facts register; classify as supported,
contradicted, stale (expired) or no source found.
6. Vision checks: vision model plus simple image processing for logo presence, distortion,
clear space and palette adherence against asset-library references.
7. Merge and rank: deduplicate, assign severity from rule metadata, attach citations, and
store findings with the guideline version used.
8. Calibration: keep a labelled test set (target 50–100 items) and report precision and
recall for each category; tune thresholds before demo.

7. Responsible Design: Privacy, Security, Oversight
7.1 Human oversight
AI never publishes, deletes or overrides. It only flags and suggests.
High-risk categories (health, finance, legal, children) always require an authorised
reviewer.
Every override needs a reason and is logged. Disputing a flag is one click and feeds the
insights loop.
Confidence levels shown; low-confidence findings labelled advisory.
7.2 Privacy and confidentiality
Tenant isolation for all brand materials; encryption in transit and at rest; signed,
expiring file links.
Data minimisation: send only the necessary spans and retrieved rule text to the model;
redact emails, phone numbers and names where possible before external calls.
Do not use customer content to train models. Choose providers with no-training API
terms and state this in the UI.
Retention controls and delete-on-request for content and brand packs.
Local-only mode (rules and local models) for highly confidential content.
India context: the DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified in November 2025 and are being
phased in over roughly 18 months, with full operation around May 2027. Design for
purpose limitation, notice, retention limits and breach readiness from the start, and
keep personal data in the product (users' names, emails, comments) to a minimum.

7.3 Security
Role-based access control and row-level security; audit log of reads and changes; secrets
in environment vaults; dependency scanning; prompt-injection defences (treat uploaded
content as data, never as instructions; strip or isolate embedded instructions); rate
limiting; file type and size validation.
7.4 Fairness, transparency and accessibility
Clear "AI-assisted review" label and list of external services in settings.
Style rules are the organisation's own; the tool does not invent norms. Inclusive-
language rules are configurable.
Insights default to team-level, so the product is not used to rank individuals unfairly.
Product UI follows WCAG 2.2 AA basics; findings are never conveyed by colour alone.
